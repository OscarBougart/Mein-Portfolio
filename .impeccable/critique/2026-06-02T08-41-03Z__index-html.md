## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment: Pass, and stronger than last run.** The projects section got the skim layer it needed (filter chips by type plus a one-line tagline leading each project), so a busy reader can scan and dive instead of grinding through seven detailed blocks. Golden Hour's colored placeholders are gone, so the work section no longer ends on an unfinished note. The voice is now consistent (em dashes removed across project copy, title, and footer). It still commits to a specific point of view and does not read as generated.

**Deterministic scan (detect.mjs):** 1 finding, down from 2. `single-font` is the same false positive (the detector reads only index.html and misses the Playfair display face applied via `--font-heading` in style.css; the page does pair serif + sans). The `em-dash-overuse` finding from last run is resolved (0 in body copy now), which confirms the copy pass.

**Visual overlays:** Not available. No browser automation in this session, so this is the deterministic scan plus design review.

## Overall Impression

This is now an Excellent-band portfolio. The single biggest weakness from the last critique (the projects section as an un-skimmable wall ending on placeholders) is fixed: the filter and taglines turn it into something a recruiter can navigate in seconds, and removing Golden Hour tightened both consistency and the emotional arc. What remains is genuinely optional polish, not repair. The two real opportunities left are both about payoff: the prominent audience switcher still only swaps one hero sentence, and the contact section still ends on a bare form rather than a confident close.

## What's Working

1. **The skim layer is real product thinking, not decoration.** Type filter plus a one-line tagline per project lets a recruiter filter to "Mobile" or "Full Stack," scan the taglines, and dive only where relevant. It is keyboard-accessible, announces its result count, and stays on-brand (squared chips, mono labels, single accent).
2. **The about narrative is still the emotional peak.** The hospitality-to-developer, multi-country story reads as a specific person, exactly the differentiator PRODUCT.md named.
3. **Craft and consistency are high.** Real imagery on every project, unified voice, AA contrast throughout, restrained dark system. The page rewards a close look.

## Priority Issues

### [P3] The audience switcher under-delivers on its prominence
- **Why it matters:** The Recruiter / Freelance tabs are a centered, prominent control, but they only swap one hero sentence and the CTA. The payoff is small for the visual weight, and now that there is also a project filter nearby, the page has two parallel segmentation controls that do not talk to each other.
- **Fix:** Make the audience switch drive more: change the contact framing per audience, or have "Freelance" pre-filter or reorder the projects. Connect it to the filter rather than running two separate controls.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable craft

### [P3] The contact section ends flatly
- **Why it matters:** For the freelance-client reader, the conversion moment is a bare form and social icons, with no reassurance about working with CarbonElk (no testimonial, no "what I build," no engagement framing). Peak-end rule: the end is doing less than it could.
- **Fix:** Add a short, confident lead-in to the contact section (one line of copy on what a project with CarbonElk looks like) or a small piece of social proof.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable craft

### [P3] Form error prevention and input purpose
- **Why it matters:** The form relies on native on-submit validation with no inline feedback as the user types, and the fields still lack autocomplete tokens (WCAG 1.3.5), so mobile autofill is heuristic rather than declared. Low impact, but it is the last accessibility and prevention gap.
- **Fix:** Add autocomplete="name" / "email" / "tel" and optional inline validation hints.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable harden

## Persona Red Flags

**The Recruiter (scanning to hire):** Now well served. Pre-selected Recruiter tab, one-click CV, and the new filter plus taglines let them surface relevant work in seconds. Remaining friction is minor: the audience tab and the type filter are two separate controls in close proximity, and a hurried user may not immediately grasp that they do different things.

**The Freelance Client (evaluating a studio to pay):** The filtered, real-imagery project section reads as polished and capable. The gap is the close: the contact section gives them a form but no reason-to-believe beyond the work itself. A line about engagement or a testimonial would carry them the last step.

**Sam (accessibility):** Strong. The new filter is keyboard-operable with aria-pressed state, a live result count, and focus rings, on top of the earlier focus, label, contrast, and reduced-motion work. The only remaining gap is the missing autocomplete tokens on the form.

## Minor Observations

- The audience tabs (Recruiter / Freelance) and the project filter (by type) are two segmentation controls near each other; consider distinguishing them visually or in label so visitors do not conflate them.
- The hero still has two h1 elements in the DOM (one per panel; the inactive one is display:none). Consider a single h1.
- No scroll-progress cue on what is still a long single-page scroll; minor, but it would orient first-time visitors.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the audience switch and the project filter be connected, so choosing "Freelance" actually reshapes what the visitor sees, instead of two parallel controls?
- What would a warmer, more confident close at the contact section look like, beyond a bare form?
- Does the hero need two h1 elements, or would one cleaner heading serve the dual-audience content better?
