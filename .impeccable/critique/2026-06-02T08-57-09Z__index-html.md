## Anti-Patterns Verdict

## Overall Impression

This is a finished, Excellent-band portfolio. The last substantive design opportunity (a prominent audience switcher that only swapped one sentence) is resolved: choosing Recruiter or Freelance now reframes the narrative from hero to projects to the contact close, and the recruiter path resurfaces the CV at the conversion moment. What remains is a single optional polish item on the form. There is no longer a structural or experiential weakness to fix.

## What's Working

1. **The audience switch is now a real spine for the page.** It sets one state that reframes three sections, so a recruiter and a client read meaningfully different narratives over the same work, without hiding any of it. The type filter handles granular browsing alongside it.
2. **The projects section reads fast and finishes strong.** Filter by type, scan one-line taglines, dive selectively, and every project ends on real imagery (Golden Hour's placeholders are gone).
3. **The about narrative remains the emotional peak**, and the contact section now has a directed close per audience rather than a bare form.

## Priority Issues

### [P3] Form error prevention and input purpose
- **Why it matters:** The form relies on native on-submit validation with no inline feedback as the user types, and the fields lack autocomplete tokens (WCAG 1.3.5), so mobile autofill is heuristic rather than declared. This is the only remaining concrete gap, and it is low impact.
- **Fix:** Add autocomplete="name" / "email" / "tel" to the fields, and optionally light inline validation hints.
- **Suggested command:** $impeccable harden

## Persona Red Flags

**The Recruiter (scanning to hire):** Fully served. Pre-selected Recruiter framing, the CV one click away in both the hero and the contact close, and the filter plus taglines to surface relevant work fast. No red flags remain.

**The Freelance Client (evaluating a studio to pay):** The filtered, real-imagery work reads as polished, and the contact section now greets them with a directed invitation ("Ein Projekt im Kopf? Erzähl mir davon") instead of a bare form. The reason-to-believe is stronger; the only further lift would be optional social proof.

**Sam (accessibility):** Strong throughout. Keyboard-operable filter and tabs with state, visible focus, AA contrast, live status region, reduced motion. The single remaining gap is the missing autocomplete tokens.

## Minor Observations

- The hero still has two h1 elements in the DOM (one per audience panel; the inactive one is display:none). A single h1 would be cleaner semantically.
- No scroll-progress cue on a long single-page scroll, though the active sidebar link already communicates location.

## Questions to Consider

- Is there appetite for one piece of social proof (a short testimonial) to push the freelance-client conversion further, or is the directed contact close enough?
- Does the hero need two h1 elements, or would one heading serve the dual-audience content better?
